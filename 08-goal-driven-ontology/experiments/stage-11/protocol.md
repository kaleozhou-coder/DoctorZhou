# Stage 11 protocol, 2026-11-16
Synthetic cross-document policy extraction plus downstream CQ decisions, with identical final materials, questions and output contract. Materials, contract, gold and protocol are frozen after independent isolated AI review and before any extraction arm. Three arms: one full-material pass; question-guided fixed batches (D1/D2 then D3/D4); strong full-material two-pass self-review. All second rounds use the same full final materials, questions and contract. Preserve first-round answers. Fixed batching is not autonomous retrieval. Arms are fork-none internal AI agents, not OS sandboxes; no credentials, external paid API or local service. No model-version, seed, token or compute-budget matching is available. Internal inference is not free computing.

Primary metric: exact answer match on 12 orientation/definition CQs and 16 policy decisions, separately reported, with no permissive requires/suffices paraphrase matching. False closes: ALLOW when gold is not ALLOW. Also report false blocks and UNKNOWN when gold allows. JSON/schema failures count as failures rather than being repaired. Provenance is separately assessed by an isolated AI reviewer after label freeze; do not equate a valid-looking citation to entailment. Model conclusions remain proposed. Human approval count is 0; human time, token usage and monetary cost are unmeasured/null. No claims of superiority or real-world validation are justified by one run on four synthetic documents sharing one policy domain. The author knows the labels; label review is AI independence, not human ground truth.

The output contract intentionally supplies answer categories. Therefore this is a bounded extraction/workflow test, not unconstrained ontology induction. Downstream labels expose logical errors but do not measure general graph/schema quality. Frozen output replay is deterministic; fresh model inference is not promised reproducible. Tests should reject orientation flips, UNKNOWN-to-ALLOW mistakes, duplicate rows and missing source references.
